01
SUTD · Architecture Core Studio Exercise 01 · Paper tectonics One sheet, 250 × 250 mm

Flow

Flow begins with a single 250 mm sheet of paper and three permitted operations: cuts, folds and creases. The surface is divided into a field of strips whose widths drift wider toward one edge, then each strip is pushed to the limit of how many folds and splits one plane can carry. What results reads as topography: a folded terrain that ripples from tight pleats into open ground, generated entirely from one flat sheet. Scaled up, the same logic reads as a canopy of shifting light and shadow.

SUTD · Architecture Core Studio Exercise 02 · Light & section Positive & negative massing models

Eclipse

Eclipse is an inhabitable void carved beneath a sweeping landform. Curved trapezoidal apertures open through the terrain above, each one tuned to draw daylight into the wide gathering space below. As the sun moves across the sky, the skylights cast slow, shifting crescents through the interior, turning the section itself into a kind of sundial. Developed through paired positive and negative models, the project treats emptiness, rather than mass, as its primary material.

03
SUTD · Architecture Core Studio Exercise 03 · Geometry & accessibility Circle · Square · Equilateral triangle Client · Retired gardener, 65

Gardenáf

Gardenáf answers a single client: a 65-year-old woman who has chosen a slower life around her garden and needs a home she can move through without stairs or strain. The plan is built from three primitives, a circle, a square and an equilateral triangle, then lifted into massing. The triangle anchors the composition while the circle and square shift in size and position until the figure resolves into four corner rooms around a central circle. That circle becomes the kitchen and the heart of the house, opening straight onto the garden, with every room reached from it on one level. The square is subtracted to leave a sheltered corner for parking, close at hand for limited mobility. Rigorous geometry, turned entirely toward care.

The client

I'm 65, and I've chosen to live alone in a quiet cottage, away from the rush of the city. My hip isn't as strong as it was, so I move carefully and avoid stairs and long walks, but I'm determined to stay independent. Gardening gives my days structure: I tend my herbs and vegetables each morning and cook with what I've grown. On weekends my children and grandchildren visit, and I cook for them from my own harvest. The garden is my pride, my routine, and the heart of my home.

04
SUTD · Architecture Core Studio Exercise 04 · Museum of human & AI art Queen Elizabeth Walk, Singapore Reference · MoMA

Whatthe?! · Chaos in Contrast

Whatthe?! is a museum for human- and AI-made art, sited along Queen Elizabeth Walk in Singapore and developed in dialogue with MoMA's curatorial model. The brief is easy to state and hard to resolve: house two practices under one roof without pretending they are the same. The project sets human and machine as deliberate opposites, distinct wings with distinct temperaments, then dissolves the divide toward a shared central ground where the two meet and argue. Undulating shells rise from the contoured site as that argument made built form.

Spatial Design World

A spatial design module taught through Rhino, set as a series of creative scenarios staged in our own rooms. Three exercises (floor plan, interior isometric and block axonometric) follow the same cast: my room and my dog.

2.1

Spatial Design World · Exercise 1 · Floor plan

A Crappy Situation

Exercise one asks for a floor plan of a house and the life lived inside it. Mine includes a dog. The plan documents what sharing a home with a pet actually involves, down to the daily negotiations over territory, while the animation makes the point directly: my dog, my garden, and a mess to clean up on a regular basis. A crappy situation, literally.

2.2

Spatial Design World · Exercise 2 · Interior isometric

Two's a Crowd

Exercise two re-runs the same scenario in a tiny apartment room rather than a landed house. The interior isometric resolves the logistics in full: belongings hung on the wall and out of paw's reach, a step that lets the dog reach the bed, and a dedicated bed and toys so she has a space of her own. The animation works through how the room is packed and rearranged around her. Two residents, one room, every element earning its place.

2.3

Spatial Design World · Exercise 3

Block Axonometric Perspective

The final exercise zooms out from the room to the entire block. The brief: model the surrounding neighbourhood as a set of legible solids, cutting doors, windows, kerbs and street lines just deep enough to anchor the room in its urban fabric, then use colour to pick out a single slice so it reads against the muted city around it. Here that slice is my own apartment, a single shaded window marking exactly where the earlier room sits. Private interior and public city, finally drawn at one scale.

01

Spatial Design World · Design Thinking Project III · Group project

Lattice Help You

★ Top Project in 01.020 Design Thinking Project III Showcase 2025

★ Best Project in 10.024 Spatial Design World

Lattice Help You is a portable exhibition system for a nomadic age. The brief called for a display device that could be deployed, collapsed and reused repeatedly, carrying a printed poster and physical models into changing public settings and set up by one or two people without machinery. The response is a scissor-lattice that folds flat in seconds and opens into a full display wall, built almost entirely from waste and offcuts salvaged from SUTD's fabrication labs and refined with 3D-printed and CNC parts: resource circularity as method rather than afterthought. It was built and tested at full scale as a working prototype.

02

Spatial Design World · Exercise 4 · Group project · Cabin retreat

Lemmein House

Lemmein House is a cabin conceived as a retreat from screens, deadlines and city noise. The brief sets it on a terraced slope in rural Ubud, Bali, within a strict twelve-metre cube opening onto rice paddies, mist and sky: a minimal envelope for sleeping, simple cooking and contemplation, where isolation and sensory reset are the point. We read that as a study in seclusion, a façade that stays closed and cool from the slope, then turns warm and open within. The massing is generated from three extruded lines, the roof cut into sloped planes, and one façade resolved as a lattice that filters the tropical light. Developed as a team from first sketch to a complete drawing set: storey plans, renders and a fully resolved exploded axonometric.

04

Material Studies

Structures and digital design fabrication: making at the scale of the body, in brass, timber, steel and sailcloth.

Sail

Retractable canopy · Bamboo & fabric · Five-week group build

Five weeks, a bundle of bamboo poles and a single run of fabric. Looking for a way to hold a large canopy aloft with almost no material, we kept returning to sailboats, where rigging, mast and cloth carry vast surfaces in pure tension. That logic became the structure, and the name. Sail fans open like canvas catching wind, shades the lawn beneath it, and folds away as quickly as it deploys.

Vine

Brass ring · Individual, lathe-turned

Turned from a solid brass cylinder on the lathe and finished by hand: an open band ending in two veined leaves, as though a vine had wound around the finger and stayed. Architecture at its smallest scale, where precision, patience and ornament are the whole brief.

Drift

Stool · Group build, wood & steel

A triangular seat carved into soft curves, dynamic from across the room and quietly ergonomic the moment you sit. Hand-shaped timber meets a welded steel base in a study of how far comfort and tension can share a single object.

Global Exchange

Design beyond campus. Two exchanges: one reading a city's modernist past, the other building its digital future.

Bangkok, Thailand · Modernism study trip

Reading Bangkok

A week treating the city as a living textbook, tracing how modernism reached Bangkok, adapted to its climate and culture, and left its mark on shells, vaults and sun-broken façades. Standing inside buildings we had only seen in lectures turned theory into instinct; every photograph here is a footnote made physical.

Surabaya, Indonesia · PCU × SUTD · Global Exploration Opportunities 2026

Surabaya

A cross-university design-AI programme with Petra Christian University, working directly with a local koperasi, a clothes-making cooperative. We built a machine-learning-backed web platform to organise and track their inventory, clearing the bottleneck between stockroom and sewing table so production could scale. The platform was designed and built during the closing hackathon, where it took my team 1st runner-up.

About

I'm Kaitlyn Cheng, an Architecture and Sustainable Design undergraduate at the Singapore University of Technology and Design, working on the principle that the best ideas are tested with the hands.

My work moves across scales: a brass ring turned on a lathe, a stool you can actually sit on, a folding lattice that took top project at its design-thinking showcase, and a museum imagined for Singapore's waterfront. Whether the medium is paper, timber, machine learning or light, I look for the point where a strict constraint becomes an unexpected freedom.
